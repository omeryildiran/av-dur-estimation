Duration judgments with conflicting audiovisual cues

Ömer F. Yildiran1, Long Ni1,2, Michael S. Landy1,2

1Department of Psychology  ·  2Center for Neural Science  ·  New York University

paper & code
TL;DR
Auditory duration judgments are pulled linearly toward visual duration; the bias scales with auditory noise but shows no non-linear breakdown even at ±250 ms conflicts. With sub-second AV durations, forced fusion, causal inference, and probabilistic cue switching make nearly identical predictions.
11 participants · 4 tasks · 5 models
2,156 bimodal trials / subj
01

Question

Audiovisual duration cues integrate optimally under small conflicts (Hartcher-O'Brien et al., 2014). Does causal inference break that integration when AV conflicts grow large — the way it does in spatial localization (Körding et al., 2007)?

  • Does the brain fuse, segregate, or switch between AV duration cues at large conflict?
  • How does auditory reliability shape the visual bias on perceived auditory duration?
  • Can behavioral fits actually discriminate these computational strategies?
We test 7 AV-duration conflicts (±250 ms) at two auditory-noise levels and adjudicate forced fusion, three causal-inference variants, and a heuristic cue-switching model.

Unimodal auditory & visual. Auditory: noise burst in continuous background (low- vs high-SNR). Visual: filled-disk interval against a persistent outline. Calibrates per-modality sensory noise.

B · Bimodal AV conflict

Bimodal main task. Conflict applied only to the visual standard (7 levels: −250, −167, −83, 0, +83, +167, +250 ms). Auditory durations unchanged. Per-subject AV bias compensated. Judge: which interval was longer (auditory)?

05

Models

shared encoder · 5 decoders
Sensory encoding — log-Gaussian, scalar variability
p(ma | sa) ~ 𝒩(log sa, σa2)     p(mv | sv) ~ 𝒩(log sv, σv2)

Gaussian noise in log-space ⇒ log-normal in linear time ⇒ Weber-like scalar variability. All 5 candidate models share this encoder; they differ only in how they decode ma, mv into a duration estimate.

Decoding strategies — 5 candidate models
M1 Forced fusion 4 params

Always integrate auditory + visual; reliability-weighted average. Special case of CI with pc = 1.

linear bias scaled by the variance ratio — no breakdown.
ŝa = wa ma + wv mv,  wi ∝ 1/σi2
M2 CI · Model averaging 5 params

Posterior-weighted average of fused (C=1) and segregated (C=2) estimates — graded transition between fusion and segregation.

smooth non-linear roll-off as conflict grows — magnitude controlled by pc.
ŝa = P(C=1) ŝa,C=1 + (1−P(C=1)) ŝa,C=2
M3 CI · Model selection 5 params

Pick the more probable causal hypothesis on each trial — sharp categorical transition between integration and segregation.

step-like switch as P(C=1) crosses ½.
ŝa = ŝa,C=1  if P(C=1) > ½,  else ŝa,C=2
M4 CI · Probability matching 5 params

Stochastically sample fusion or segregation per trial, with probability proportional to its causal posterior.

trial-to-trial mixture; suboptimal but psychologically plausible.
fuse ~ P(C=1),  segregate ~ 1−P(C=1)
M5 Probabilistic cue switching 6 params

Heuristic: each trial use auditory or visual cue alone, with fixed probability pv per noise condition. No causal inference.

linear, fusion-like behavior emerges from averaging across trials.
decide on mv w.p. pv; on ma w.p. 1−pv
Fitting. Maximum-likelihood via BADS (Acerbi & Ma, 2017) with 10 random restarts; per-condition response probabilities estimated from 2000 Monte Carlo trials per cell. Compared by AIC and random-effects BMS (PXP, BOR).

Main result · PSE shifts

Visual bias on perceived auditory duration grows approximately linearly with conflict and is much larger under high auditory noise. The non-linear breakdown predicted by causal inference is not observed.

Why models converge

posterior of common cause

With realistic sensory noise, P(C=1 | ma, mv) stays above 0.5 across the entire ±250 ms range. CI behaves like fusion → distinct strategies make near-identical predictions.

09

Conclusion

  • Auditory duration is biased toward vision; bias scales with auditory noise.
  • Bias grows linearly with conflict — no causal-inference breakdown observed.
  • Heuristic cue switching fits marginally best, but model evidence is essentially flat.
  • High encoding noise keeps P(C=1) high → fundamental identifiability limit for sub-second AV duration tasks.
  • Causal inference may still be plausible; behavior alone cannot adjudicate within experimentally tolerable conflict ranges.
